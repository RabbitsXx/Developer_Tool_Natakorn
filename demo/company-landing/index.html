What differs, and why it matters

  • Copy leads with the company ("Automate Your Invoices") and hype claims with no source
  • Fake urgency: countdown timer that resets forever, "50% OFF" that applies to nothing
  • Fake precision: 12,847 customers, 99.99% uptime, "$4.2M saved" — invented on the spot
  • Signup form "validates" by printing success on any input, including empty text
  • Fixed 1200px container — breaks on a phone; buttons are divs with onclick
  • Copy leads with the user's job ("follows up so you don't have to"); every number is scoped and modest
  • No dark patterns: the free plan is stated as non-expiring; the product's limits are stated outright
  • Pricing is a real comparison table with limits per plan, not three mystery boxes
  • Form has a labeled input, inline validation, role="alert" error, focus management, aria-invalid
  • Fluid layout, skip link, semantic headings, keyboard-visible focus states

Verdict

The difference isn't visual taste — it's that the kit's ui-ux pack requires the page to make testable promises: a user goal in the first screen, no unverifiable numbers, a form that rejects bad input honestly, and a QA pass that actually runs before completion is claimed. The "without" page looks finished. It just isn't done.
